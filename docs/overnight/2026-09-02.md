## 1.1 Weights — the same checkpoint, which is the whole ballgame
CLIP's two towers are trained together with two learned projection matrices onto one shared space.
A text encoder from a *different* checkpoint returns 512 unit-length floats that are the right shape
and mean nothing against these images, and nothing anywhere would throw. So the text tower is the
same repo and revision as the vision tower already on disk:

| file | bytes | sha256 |
|---|---|---|
| `text_model.onnx` | 254,058,553 | `3f6571f5bad13a97c469c1622e1cfc4d9aef78b79fdbfcff804ca357bfada8cc` |
| `vocab.json` | — | `5047b556ce86ccaf6aa22b3ffccfc52d391ea4accdab9c2f2407da5b742d4363` |
| `merges.txt` | — | `9fd691f7c8039210e0fced15865466c65820d09b63988b0174bfe25de299051a` |

All three from `Xenova/clip-vit-base-patch32`, all three pinned in source and checked on load, all
three in gitignored `.models/`. **No API key, no credit, no network at query time.**

ONNX io, probed rather than assumed: **inputs `['input_ids']`, outputs `['text_embeds']`, `[n,512]`
float32.** `text_embeds` is POST-projection; reading the pooled hidden state instead (768-d) is the
standard way to get this silently wrong, and it is called out in the file.

## 1.2 There is no `attention_mask` input, and that is correct — MEASURED
The export takes `input_ids` only. That is not a defect: CLIP's text transformer is causally masked
and pools at the eos position, so nothing after the first eos can reach the vector. Rather than
argue it, `"a woodcut of a bird"` was embedded three ways — at its real length of **8 tokens**,
padded to 77 with eos, and padded to 77 with **zeros**:

```
padded-77(EOS) vs truncated : 1.00000000
padded-77(EOS) vs padded-0  : 1.00000000
```

Identical to eight decimal places. This is now a test (`embedTokens` exists only so it can be one).

## 1.3 The tokenizer was written by hand, and it is exactly right
`@huggingface/transformers` would have dragged in a **second onnxruntime** beside the
`onnxruntime-node` this repo already loads. Two native runtimes in one process is a worse problem
than 150 lines of byte-level BPE, so the BPE was written: `bytes_to_unicode`, CLIP's own split
regex, the `</w>` word-final convention, and the merge table (**49,408 vocab entries, 48,888
merges** after skipping the `#version: 0.2` header).

The positive control is exact:

```
a photo of a cat -> [ 320, 1125, 539, 320, 2368 ]   (published ids, matched)
```

Negative control: `woodcut` is **not in the vocabulary** and BPE-splits to `[3269, 3032]` rather
than vanishing into an unknown token — which is what would have happened silently, to every
printmaking query, had the merge loop been wrong. `appliqué` and `Œuvres`, both real museum medium
strings that a previous TF-IDF run mangled, survive the byte mapping and do **not** collide with
their unaccented spellings.

## 1.4 The index loader deduplicates, because Rule 7 says the loader is where it belongs
`artist/clip-index.ts` joins `corpus/clip.f32` to the manifest by sha256 and is the single door
every similarity in this stage goes through:

```
19,807 rows in the file -> 19,791 entries
   98 duplicate manifest rows collapsed (kept as `aliases`, not dropped silently)
    0 zero rows (no recorded encoder failures)
```

19,791 is the same number the deduped `resemblance` baseline reached from the other direction. The
work that names an image is the **lowest id** among those sharing the bytes, so the choice does not
depend on manifest order. Image rows sampled 100-at-a-stride are unit length to **1.000000**, so a
dot product is a cosine and nothing renormalises at query time.

## 1.7 `corpus search`, and why no raw cosine is ever printed alone
CLIP's text-image cosines live in a narrow band around 0.20-0.35 — the learned logit scale is
thrown away by the export — so "this work scored 0.33" is nearly contentless. Every hit therefore
carries its **z-score and percentile within that query's own distribution over the entire corpus**,
which costs nothing because the full scan has already computed every score. Same discipline as
`artist envelope` and `artist resemblance`, same reason: a number whose real range is 0.15-0.40,
printed as though it ran 0 to 1, is a lie with four decimal places on it.

# Stage 2 — influences: an artist's background as weights over the corpus

## 2.0 What this closes
Stage 1 made the corpus addressable in words. Stage 2 asks the first artist-shaped question of it:
**given a position file, which 48 of 19,791 works is this artist working out of?** The answer is
written to `aesthetic/influences/<id>.json` (the queries) and `<id>.resolved.json` (the works,
centroid, axes and statistics), both tracked, both content-hashed as `influencesHash`.

**Zero model calls.** The queries are cut out of the position file with a regex and a sentence
splitter; the retrieval is the local CLIP text tower from Stage 1. Nothing has *read* anything.

## 2.1 How a position becomes queries — verbatim text, no interpretation
| source | what it takes | weight | k |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lineage` | each entry's `ref` string as written | 1.0 | 12 |
| `worldview` | each sentence of the paragraph, split on `.!?` | 0.8 | 12 |
| `commitment` | each commitment's `why` | 0.6 | 8 |
| `avoid` | cliches + prohibition `why`s | down-weight, never removal | — |

Counted on disk, not assumed: **19 queries for each of the three positions** (6 lineage, 7-8
worldview, 5-6 commitment) and **8-11 for the four pack elements**. **0 queries truncated** past
CLIP's 77-token context and **0 empty** — every query returned something above `minCosineToQuery`
0.20. That the worldview sentence split was needed at all is measurable: the un-split paragraphs run
well past 77 tokens, and CLIP would have embedded the first two clauses and discarded the rest in
silence.

`avoid` is a **multiplier on weight**, `max(0.5, 1 - max(0, cos - floor) * 2.5)`, never a filter.
Penalties actually bite: the top of `interference` runs 0.71-0.86, so the prohibition text is moving
the ranking rather than decorating it.

## 2.2 THE BUG THAT MAKES THIS STAGE HONEST — the PCA was returning noise
`corpus influences resolve withheld` printed:

```
axis 0 — 10.2% of the set's variance
axis 1 — 22.0% of the set's variance
axis 2 —  4.2% of the set's variance
```

Principal axes are ordered *by construction*. Axis 1 cannot explain more than axis 0. Nothing else
in the output looked wrong — three axes, real works with plausible captions at both ends of each,
labels drawn from real catalogue columns. **The only thing that gave it away was arithmetic.**

Cause: `principalAxes` tested convergence with `1 - |dot(next, v)|` where `next` is unit-length but
the **seed `v` was not** — 512 uniform draws have norm about 6.5, so the dot product exceeded 1,
`moved` came out **negative**, and `moved < 1e-9` broke the loop after a **single power step**. Every
axis was one multiplication away from the random start vector.

Verified against ground truth rather than by inspection: the 48x48 Gram matrix run to 5,000
iterations in float64 gives `0.2747 / 0.0739 / 0.0614`; the fixed implementation gives
`0.2747 / 0.0739 / 0.0614`. Before the fix it gave `0.1212 / 0.1922 / 0.0320`.

A second defect was fixed at the same time: `principalAxes` was being handed the set's `centroid`,
which is **unit-normalised** because it doubles as a retrieval query. Centring on it is not PCA. The
mean is now computed inside the function and the parameter is gone, so the mistake is unavailable.

`artist/atlas.ts` has its own `principalAxes` and **is not affected** — it has no early-exit
convergence test and always runs all 200 iterations. The published map numbers (metadata PCA 34.0x,
CLIP PCA 5.6x) stand.

The regression test is `PCA axes are ordered — explained variance never increases`. It needs no
corpus and no ground truth, because the property is true by construction.

## 2.3 The extremes operator, and where it stops
Walk out from the centroid along each axis in steps of half the set's own radius, and ask the corpus
what is nearest at each step. The walk stops when the nearest work falls below the corpus's own
median pair similarity (0.6428), and a 12-step cap guarantees termination regardless.

Which of the two stopped it is now **recorded and printed** (`endedPlus` / `endedMinus`), because it
changes what the output means. Before the PCA fix, walks along noise directions mostly ran into the
step cap; with real axes, **all six walks on `withheld` terminate on the corpus hull** (3-10 steps).
A walk that ends on the cap is reporting a direction the corpus keeps answering, and should be read
differently from one that ran out of corpus.

## 2.7 What this stage does NOT establish
- **This is retrieval, not reading.** A lineage entry names a real work that is almost certainly not
  in this corpus. What comes back is whatever *photographs like the words in its title*. The
  `withheld` set is full of Qur'an folios and papyrus fragments because its worldview says
  "redaction", "screen" and "withheld" — not because anything considered redaction.
- **No artist has used one of these sets yet.** That is Stage 5, and the model-calling half of it is
  `BLOCKED: credit`.
- **48 is a chosen number**, not a discovered one, and so are k=12/k=8 and the 1.0/0.8/0.6 weights.
  Nothing here has been tuned against an outcome, because there is no outcome to tune against yet.
